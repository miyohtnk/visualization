      Good visualization citation: https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/neil.richards/viz/globaltemperatures/Dashboard1
In class, we learned that a good visual should not require too much thinking to be understood (working memory is limited), that there should be a ‘bigger picture’ showing overall trends that then zooms into the nitty gritty of the data, the values and details. I think a good visual (especially in science) should not manipulate the data to further a cause (by emphasizing a certain emotion), but rather, it should be as neutral as possible and allow the viewer to freely place meaning on the data as they see fit. Different groups could use the same data to push ideologies, and although the data shown may not be dishonest per se, changing the presentation of the data can change the narrative. A visual can also be persuasive, depending on the audience. A good visual should cite at least one source for the data used, be aesthetically pleasing, accurately present the data, and it should be easily understood. It should be made with the intended audience in mind (the viewers’ age, education, expertise and accessibility).
In the good visualization that I picked from Tableau (see link), the visualization type is easy to comprehend- blue is low, red is high. This is the standard in heatmaps, and they have stuck to the same format. There is a large visual element (the graph), which reduces the cognitive load, whilst keeping all the required information in. The title is clear (“Global Temperature change 1850-2016), and it breaks down the rows into decades, showing a warming trend as the decades pass. Specific numbers per year are not required for the “overall message” to be understood- our planet is warming. There is also the possibility to zoom in on the data to see how each year differed from the reference point. The scale is understandable (-1 to 1.1 degrees C), and the lower/higher confidence levels, including the median, are reported- showing us some more metrics. The source has been cited. The assumption here is that the intended audience is the general adult population, so the language is appropriate. Based on Kennedy et al. (2016), a good visualization should adhere to four conventions of data, which this graph follows: 1. It is a two-dimensional image; the layout is clean, with clear borders for the heatmap; the geometric shapes and lines are standard, and the horizontal lines are used to easily convey scale; there is inclusion of the data source (albeit at the top of the image instead of the bottom).

Bad visualization: https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/docinsight/viz/RadialMarimekko/RM
On the other hand, a bad visualization is confusing, excessive, has high cognitive load, and does not follow the gestalt principles, or the framework proposed by Kennedy et al. In the visual that I picked (bad visualization link), at a glance the graph is nice- however, there are a number of issues to discuss. The biggest part of the visual cannot be interacted with (the globe), so a lot of the space has been taken up for no good reason. The line height for each country seems to reflect the CO2 per capita, but this visual is not the best- it is hard to compare the scale of two lines if they are not next to each other (and also not as a spiral). The categorization of continents (this is the assumption but it is not stated) is strange, especially when one sees that Kazakhstan has been placed under “Europe”, when it is clearly an Asian country. The lack of any visual data, or scale makes it hard to understand at a first glance. Based on Kennedy et al. (2016) principles: although this is a 2D visual, it almost has a 3D feel to it, making it confusing; the layout is not very clean- the lines and the different colours make it “noisy”, as well as the fact that the circle is not “complete” (is that part of the world empty?); the geometric shapes do not make sense in their axes- what does width mean versus height?; the data is accurately mentioned at the bottom of the page.

The bad visualization could be improved if instead of a pie/bar chart style, the data was shown spatially on a map. If color saturation was used, in combination with luminance, it would make the visual much easier to understand. In addition, if some text was used to give context to the figure, measurement standards etc (How are CO2 emissions reported), it would make it easier for the audience to understand. Better analysis of the data would make the graph clearer ( for example, what is “Asia A”, “Middle East B”), make it easier to search for each country with a search bar that opens to more information. Reporting the year, or having a 5-year period of emissions would make it more accurate, because the CO2 output of each country changes every year.
In the good visualization, I would also have a search bar, in order to make it easier to select the desired time period of interest (hovering over a year is hard when there is so much data), have the citation at the bottom of the page.
